# Judge queue estimates

`check_before_done` returns judge queue telemetry in MCP `structuredContent.queue` and explains it in the text response. It includes capacity (`CHECK_CONCURRENCY`, default 2 judge calls at once), live running/waiting counts, the request's current waiting position (1 is next), checks ahead on arrival, whether the request still holds a slot, the position a new retry would currently take, and a retry delay when appropriate.

Estimates cover judge admission and service, not embedding, store access or the entire task. `estimate.startMs` and `completionMs` are millisecond windows measured from the response snapshot. `estimate.scope` is `judge`. `state` is `waiting`, `running`, `completed`, or `retry_required`.

The queue keeps up to 64 actual service durations. A duration ends only when a running call really releases its slot, including when its caller already timed out. Queued requests cancelled before running contribute no duration and are removed immediately. With observations, the lower/upper service scenarios use the 25th/90th percentiles (with fewer than ten observations, 2.5 times the slowest one stands in for the 90th) and simulate FIFO service across the configured concurrency, subtracting the elapsed time of active calls. These are estimates, not confidence intervals or guaranteed deadlines. Recent model speed and future demand can differ.

At cold start, `estimate.source` is `response_budget` and `samples` is zero. The window uses zero to the configured response budget per service wave; this is an explicitly labeled scheduling scenario, not evidence that a model will release a slot by that time. Once actual calls finish, `source` becomes `observed`. When an active call exceeds the observed service range, its lane's upper scenario is its own service deadline. `overdue` becomes true only after that deadline, and only then is the lane unavailable in the upper scenario. Healthy or free lanes still provide a finite scheduling estimate; the upper endpoint is `null` only when no such lane can serve the request. An uncooperative call continues occupying real capacity. The `running` notification is emitted when a call starts; its window is measured at that admission snapshot, not a later progress update.

A check still obeys its end-to-end response deadline. A timed-out request has `retained: false`, `position: null`, and `state: retry_required`. It is not a background check and holds no physical queue slot. When an authenticated check's judge call had already started, that call keeps running for an identical retry to join: the response carries `inFlight.elapsedMs`, its estimate is the call's remaining service (start `[0, 0]`), and `retryAfterMs` follows its remaining median service rather than a fresh place in the queue. A running call's remaining service is read from the observed calls that lasted longer than it has run so far (their p25, median and p90, less its elapsed time), so a call far into its service is never estimated from the fast calls that finished; one that has run longer than every observed call is estimated as long again as it has run, up to its own service deadline. Once ten calls have been observed, the queue also decides from them whether a call can finish in the response time left: an authenticated check whose remaining response budget is below the median service starts its judge call and answers "queued" at once instead of waiting out a budget the call cannot fit, and an unauthenticated one that waited until even the 25th-percentile service no longer fits is not started (it would be cancelled at the response deadline). Authenticated callers can retain retry priority as described below. `nextRetryPosition` and the estimate describe where that retry would enter the live queue at that moment; `retryAfterMs` is a pacing suggestion between 1 and 60 s derived from the estimated next start, not a reservation or promise. A retry starts with a fresh response budget. A queued or not-judged answer is not a completed check, so the Stop hook continues to block completion. Its text is for the agent: the lessons the check was about to judge, to check by hand meanwhile, and one line, "Not finished: ... Call check_before_done again with the same arguments in about N s."; the numbers above are in the structured `queue` only.

The response also gives a median-service scenario alongside the wider interval when observations are available. Completed checks show measured waiting and service durations rather than a meaningless 0–0 second ETA. An unusually slow but still-in-budget call does not produce the warning for a call past its response deadline. Retries start a new request with a fresh response budget. When an authenticated check times out before its judge starts, the server retains its original queue ordinal for two minutes. A matching retry joins ahead of newer waiting checks, without reserving capacity while absent. `retryPriority` reports whether priority is retained, restored, and its remaining lifetime. The text response reports this separately from the physical queue position.

Priority is bound to the validated caller credential/capability, run, project, session, task, changed files, actual diff and selected lessons. Different callers or changed checks cannot reuse it. Unauthenticated requests have no retained priority. The ordinal stays the original, but each timed-out retry renews its two minutes from that attempt; concurrent duplicates cannot take the same reservation. The process keeps at most 1,024 reservations, evicts the oldest when full, and loses reservations on restart. Expiry, eviction or restart simply returns the retry to ordinary FIFO order. Unauthenticated running calls receive cancellation on timeout. An authenticated call that already started is not cancelled at the response deadline: it runs while identical retries keep coming back for it, up to `CHECK_WAIT_SECONDS` (default 300 s, its service deadline), and is abandoned two minutes after the response deadline of the last request waiting on it. An identical retry joins it, and its verdict is kept for two minutes. A call that runs to its service deadline is a failed check, and that failure is kept for two minutes too, so the retries in that window are told so (with the lessons to check by hand) instead of starting a call bound to overrun the same way; a call that fails otherwise (a provider error) is dropped at once, so the next retry starts a fresh call. A joiner released because its queued original expired keeps its own arrival time and remaining response budget.

Completed responses use `estimate.source: measured`: `startMs` is the actual waiting duration and `completionMs` is waiting plus actual service, both measured from that request's judge-queue arrival. These are historical timings, not a future 0–0 ETA. `observed.waitedMs` and `serviceMs` remain available separately. `observed.shared` is `joined` when the request waited on an identical call already in progress (its service is the time until that result arrived) and `reused` when it received a kept verdict at once; neither made a judge call of its own, and the text response says so. If an active call has already run longer than the observed median, the median scenario allows another median service interval instead of predicting an immediate release; tail estimates remain uncertain.

Every selected-project check attempt records an outcome: `lesson_judged`, `miss_judged`, `no_applicable_rules`, `queued`, or `failed`. Only lesson judgments enter the approved-lesson catch-rate denominator. The check reserves up to two seconds (at most a tenth) of its response budget, 50 s or `CHECK_WAIT_SECONDS` when lower, to save the outcome. `recorded: false` means the outcome could not be saved before that deadline (for example, the store was unavailable); it must not be interpreted as a saved check. `recorded: null` means recording was intentionally skipped because the project was unselected or unknown; those inputs are never retained.
